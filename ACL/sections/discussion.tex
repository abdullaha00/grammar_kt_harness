\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{RQ1: conservative normalization exposes the bottleneck.}
Only 31.7\% of sampled descriptors supported exact cells.  Phase~2 resolved two
of 80 partial cases, so examples rarely licensed a safe refinement under the
transition rules.  This is not merely low recall: retaining 77 partial mappings
prevents the system from manufacturing combinations that the source does not
establish.  Exact agreement on eight repeats is encouraging but too small, and
too dependent on one unpinned model service, to substitute for expert review.

\paragraph{RQ2: policies encode different structural assumptions.}
Factorized KCs reuse evidence but produce multi-KC rows.  Whole cells eliminate
overlap at the cost of 24 narrowly supported categories.  Interactions increase
row width and, in one case, duplicate an existing column on the observed
domain.  The harness makes such redundancy visible before a representation is
used with learners.

\paragraph{RQ3: integration success is not cognitive validation.}
All policy artifacts support leakage-audited simulation and three KT baselines;
within each, logistic regression is the strongest technical baseline.  But the
simulator defines mastery and responses using the same KC assignments later
given to the models.  Moreover, five items per KC creates different datasets.
Predictive differences are therefore conditional on each synthetic world, not
evidence that a policy captures human acquisition.  A valid policy comparison
requires a shared, independently labelled item set and real learner sequences.
