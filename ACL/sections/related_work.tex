\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Grammar resources and opportunity boundaries.}
The CEFR provides proficiency scales, while the English Grammar Profile (EGP)
links corpus-supported grammar competence statements to CEFR levels
\citep{coe-2020-cefr,okeeffe-mark-2017}.  These are rich linguistic resources,
not item banks or cognitive models.  TSCCv2 retains discourse relations,
pedagogical focus, repair, and correction in real English tutoring
\citep{caines-etal-2022-teacher}; dialogue KT must infer which turns are
opportunities and what each tests \citep{scarlatos-etal-2025-dialogue}.  We
study the complementary setting in which opportunity and generator truth are
declared, then ask how much ecological context can be added before that
boundary becomes ambiguous.

\paragraph{KC/Q specification and identifiability.}
KCs connect performance to instructional units \citep{koedinger-etal-2012}.
Classical BKT models learning, guess, and slip
\citep{corbett-anderson-1995}; PFA uses logistic practice features
\citep{pavlik-etal-2009}.  Cognitive-model refinement combines statistical
evidence with expert interpretation \citep{cen-etal-2006}, and response data can
validate or refine Q \citep{de-la-torre-chiu-2016,pardos-dadu-2018}.  Q
misspecification can be absorbed into response parameters and damage latent
classification \citep{rupp-templin-2008}.  Formal Q-identifiability conditions
are response-model-specific; for example, DINA results require structure beyond
numerical rank \citep{gu-xu-2021}.  We therefore separate linear Q geometry,
empirical recovery, pedagogical interpretation, and psychological truth.

\paragraph{Measurement validity, item difficulty, and format.}
Validity concerns the interpretation and use of responses, including construct
underrepresentation and construct-irrelevant variance
\citep{messick-1994,kane-2013}.  Item difficulty is central in IRT and has been
incorporated into KT \citep{pardos-heffernan-2011}; individualized KT shows that
learner learning rates can also matter \citep{yudelson-etal-2013}.  Format
effects are heterogeneous: matched multiple-choice and constructed-response
stems are more comparable than unmatched content, and language-test effects
depend on design and proficiency \citep{rodriguez-2003,innami-koizumi-2009}.
Thus a format contrast requires matched semantic content and crossed KCs, not
unrelated item campaigns.  We use zero and planted controls rather than assign
one supposedly universal difficulty ordering to task types.

\paragraph{Platform selection and learner responses.}
SLAM includes millions of language-production tokens with exercise, time, and
error metadata \citep{settles-etal-2018}; EdNet mixes problem, lecture,
purchase, and within-question events at much larger scale
\citep{choi-etal-2020}.  Such logs are selected service histories, not random
item assignment.  Spaced-practice models make elapsed exposure part of the
learning process \citep{settles-meeder-2016}.  We compare a laboratory schedule
with simple curriculum, mixed, and observable-history adaptive alternatives,
while explicitly avoiding a policy-value claim.

Binary correctness also discards linguistic evidence.  Learner corpora and
ERRANT show that grammatical errors can be typed, while multiple valid
corrections complicate exact scoring \citep{bryant-etal-2017,bryant-etal-2019}.
We begin with structured categories and linked/shuffled information controls;
we do not call generated labels human errors or generate surface text without
validation.

\paragraph{Synthetic learner studies.}
Known truth makes controlled KT evaluation possible
\citep{piech-etal-2015,pagonis-etal-2024}, but simulator validity is
purpose-specific, and educational simulations often provide limited validity
evidence \citep{kaser-alexandron-2024}.  Our response is not one increasingly
ornate ``realistic'' simulator.  We retain a clean world and add separate paired
worlds for item, format, learner, error, and policy mechanisms, each with a
negative or positive control and a bounded claim.
