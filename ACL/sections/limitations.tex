\section*{Limitations}

The ontology covers single-clause English verbal morphosyntax: present and past
tense, four aspect values, active and canonical BE-passive voice, polarity,
five clause types, and nine central modals.  It excludes question tags, linked
conditionals, embedded questions, GET-passives, semi-modals, non-verbal grammar,
and multi-clause relations.  The stratified 139-descriptor sample is not the
full EGP and only exact mappings enter downstream stages.

Normalization and item diagnostics use one automated model backend without
expert adjudication.  Eight normalization repeats and five diagnostic repeats
are too few for general reliability claims.  The diagnostic's near-universal
acceptance is consistency evidence, not proof of naturalness or uniqueness;
the model snapshot and all decoding parameters are not pinned.  Surface
realization also uses a ten-frame lexicon and fixed transformations, limiting
lexical and discourse diversity.

Most importantly, the simulator defines responses from the same KC assignments
being evaluated, and the five-items-per-KC rule gives each policy a different
item pool.  Its outputs can expose leakage, sparsity, and software failures, but
cannot validate a policy's cognitive reality, acquisition order, or transfer to
learners.  Such claims require expert-labelled shared items and independent
human interaction data.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

No learner or human-participant records are used; all sequences and profile
labels are synthetic.  Profile names denote Beta-distribution settings, not
demographic or ability groups.  Generated items must not be deployed for
assessment or instruction without human review for correctness, ambiguity,
accessibility, and bias.  Use and quotation of the external EGP source remain
subject to its licence; the artifact stores identifiers, selection metadata,
and verification material rather than redistributing the snapshot.
