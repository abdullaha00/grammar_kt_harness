\subsection{RQ1: auditability does not imply platform validity}

\input{tables/normalization_results}

All 1,222 source rows receive a disposition.  The complete in-scope subset
yields 75 canonical cells; the outcome-independent release freezes 18 generator
factors, 113 prompts, a $113\times18$ Q with 269 edges and rank 18, and 283,000
replayable events.  This establishes artifact integrity and a usable
perfect-information control.

The stronger measurement claim fails.  In the strict prompt census, 70/113
items are usable under an assumed response-slot UI, 15 need minor repair, 15 are
answerable but artificial, 10 have a material answer-space problem, and three
require rewrite/withholding.  A four-role audit yields 452 judgments.  After a
declared mapping, the audits agree exactly on 70 items; only 60 are usable in
both, 53 occur in the union requiring action, and 18 in the critical
answer-space/withhold union.  The four roles disagree internally on 56 items.
These are model-based warnings, not estimates of human deployability.

The structural KC audit likewise narrows the generator claim.  Only 16/113
items are single-KC rows and six KCs lack an isolating opportunity.  Three
outcome-blind KC inductions produce 17/18/18 unique activation signatures with
ranks 17/18/17.  Only nine signatures are common to all three, their union has
30, pairwise Jaccard is .400--.458, and 5/4/7 exactly match \kstar{}.  The
18 columns remain a declared, useful experimental coordinate system, not a
uniquely recovered human ontology.

The matched-format construction makes the measurement gap concrete.  Across
three rounds, all 178 calls complete technically, producing 106 candidates,
712 solver attempts, and 90 role judgments.  The family funnel is
$38\rightarrow31\rightarrow12\rightarrow3$ in round 1,
$35\rightarrow32\rightarrow12\rightarrow2$ in round 2, and
$33\rightarrow26\rightarrow6\rightarrow0$ in round 3 (deterministic,
solver, critic gates).  Only 5/38 complete families (20/152 slots) pass,
covering 4/20 cells, 6/18 KCs, and seen-Q rank 3.  Measurement critics accept
9/30 critic-reached candidates versus 29/30 linguistic and 24/30 product
decisions; 23/30 decisions are mixed.  No new dataset is released.

\subsection{RQ2: representation and measurement nuisance interact}

\input{tables/kt_results}

In clean full-v1, all-probe loss is minimized at \kstar{} (.670627).  Family
merging costs .008132, split-2 .003165, split-4 .005868, and exact cells
.015039; every learner-paired interval excludes zero.  All nine frozen 10\%
Q-corruption instances are harmful.  Oracle prerequisite-state RMSE agrees
overall: .123738 for \kstar{} versus .146300 family-coarse, .132752 split-2,
.140428 split-4, and .163828 exact cells.  These are recoverability results in
the world generated from \kstar{}, not evidence that \kstar{} is human truth.

Legacy simulator controls already expose one important exception: under
unmodelled item logit difficulty, split-2 beats \kstar{} in one of three seeds.
The matched structural experiment isolates that alternative explanation.

\input{tables/measurement_nuisance_results}

The zero-format negative control behaves correctly: A/B mean loss is
.657219/.664138, so an unnecessary format split hurts.  Under the strong planted
format effect, A/B/C loss is .663597/.638965/.633648.  Thus B can win by
absorbing measurement nuisance, but the observed format model C performs
better.  The DiD is negative in all seeds (mean $-.031551$), and C-B is also
negative in all seeds (mean $-.005317$).

The result is not universal.  In the item-only world, B-A is +.001195,
+.000847, and $-.001328$; every conditional interval crosses zero, so this
experiment does not show format splits absorbing item difficulty.  In the
combined heterogeneous world, C-B is $-.002226$, +.000169, and +.001074, again
with every interval crossing zero.  D-C remains negative, but D is deliberately
aligned to the planted seen-item residual span.  It establishes a positive
control, not arbitrary or unseen-item deconfounding.

\subsection{RQ3: information and collection determine distinguishability}

\input{tables/discovery_results}

The clean observable selector recovers a high-overlap activation-equivalence
class, not a unique ontology.  Atomic and compositional policies have identical
seen Q; the compositional candidate-space ceiling recovers 18/18 generator
columns, while the selected atomic projection recovers 16/18 (activation
Jaccard .970854; aligned Q-edge F1 .965385).  Their seen predictions are
identical.  Hash distractors recover 0/18 and cost .013238, proving the
selector is informative without making equivalent rules identifiable.

Collection controls explain why.  Repeating only A+B opportunities leaves
factorised, union, interaction, and missing-interaction representations exactly
tied through $N=1000$.  A-only and B-only anchors restore rank and make the
union detectably wrong, yet a weak planted interaction remains difficult: at
$N=1000$, omitting it costs only .000506 in the balanced design.  More responses
cannot repair missing activation contrasts; numerical rank alone does not
guarantee practically unique recovery.

Structured errors supply a different kind of contrast.

The linked positive control reduces mean next-response loss by only .000867;
one of three conditional intervals crosses zero.  The 80\%-linked mean gain is
.000527 and excludes zero in one seed.  Nevertheless, failed-KC top-1 rises
from .421 under binary evidence to 1.000/.884 under exact/80\%-linked evidence.
The shuffled control does not improve prediction and localises poorly, but it
also improves the secondary evidence-count RMSE over binary attribution by
distributing multi-KC failures.  No single state metric therefore proves
learner-specific error information.

Policy changes the histories a model sees.  Curriculum-minus-lab D loss
averages +.003420 (two of three seed-conditional intervals exclude zero), mixed
practice is approximately null ($-.000018$; all cross zero), and adaptive-minus-
lab averages +.003118 (one excludes zero).  Lab, curriculum, and mixed have the
same occurrence multiset and exactly the same within-seed terminal mastery and
probe accuracy.  Their differences are history-recovery effects, not learning
efficacy.  Adaptive assignment changes exposure and state, so its difference
mixes selection, practice, outcome, and fit.

Finally, the visible dialogue continuum exposes an ecology--precision tradeoff.
Cloze receives 17/20 naturalness, 17/20 determinacy, and 17/20 clear-KC
judgments, with one shortcut and mean response-family lower bound 1.30.  Open
dialogue receives 20/20 naturalness but 0/20 determinate judgments (16 bounded,
three materially ambiguous, one not applicable), only 4/20 clear-KC judgments,
13 shortcuts, and mean response-family bound 4.55.  Open-minus-cloze matched
risk is +1.00 for determinacy, +.70 for KC attribution, +1.50 incidental
grammar, and +.60 shortcuts; response families grow by 3.25.  Dialogue
completion is not a universal compromise (14/20 shortcuts; 3/20 clear KC).
Critics disagree on KC attribution for 14/20 opportunities and platform
plausibility for 12/20.  Greater conversational naturalness can therefore
weaken the scorable opportunity boundary KT requires.

\subsection{Supporting linguistic generalisation}

\input{tables/generalisation_results}

The original known-truth benchmark still answers a question that real logs
cannot score directly.  \kstar{} loss is .669161 on seen grammar, .672036 on 15
pairwise-seen/full-tuple-unseen cells, and .681181 on six unseen-value cells;
exact-cell histories cost .037609/.028627 on the two held-out regimes.  This is
narrow compositional extrapolation under a generator whose KCs all have seen
support.  It is not unrestricted human grammatical transfer, and the
measurement audit prevents treating this clean result as a platform finding.
