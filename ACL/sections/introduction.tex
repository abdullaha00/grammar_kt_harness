\section{Introduction}

Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates changing learner knowledge from chronological
responses \citep{corbett-anderson-1995,abdelrahman-etal-2023}.  A KT dataset
normally associates each item with one or more knowledge components (KCs),
often through a binary Q-matrix.  That matrix embeds consequential scientific
decisions: what counts as reusable knowledge, which interactions require
separate state, and where learning should transfer.  It is not merely a data
format.

Those decisions are unusually difficult to audit in language learning.
Conversational corpora obscure where a grammatical opportunity begins and
which construction it measures; vocabulary-oriented benchmarks do not isolate
grammar KCs; and large tutoring logs may expose skill labels without a
transparent linguistic decomposition.  Grammar resources offer richer
descriptions, but not ready-made KT objects.  The English Grammar Profile (EGP),
for example, contains over 1,200 corpus-derived competence descriptions
\citep{okeeffe-mark-2017}.  A descriptor can combine tense, aspect, voice,
polarity, clause type, and modality, state bounded alternatives, or lie outside
a declared grammar scope.

We introduce \system, a controlled environment in which the scientific
objects and their causal order are explicit:
\begin{equation}
\grammarcell \ne \kstar \ne \khat .
\end{equation}
A \grammarcell{} is a canonical linguistic description of the construction
instantiated by an item.  The generator inventory \kstar{} and its deterministic
item mapping \qstar{} are declared synthetic truth: simulated learners have
mastery over these KCs.  A discovered or deliberately misspecified inventory
\khat{} with mapping \qhat{} is instead a downstream experimental hypothesis.
None of these objects is asserted to be the cognitive truth of human grammar.

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the resulting two-layer programme.  Layer A
processes the complete source inventory, freezes \kstar{} before learner
outcomes exist, constructs and audits a fixed measurement bank and \qstar{},
and only then simulates public responses plus separate private oracle truth.
Layer B leaves that release immutable while changing or hiding the supplied KC
representation.

We ask four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[RQ1] Can a grammar resource be transformed into an auditable fixed
    language-learning dataset with explicit KCs, items, item--KC mappings, and
    learner interactions?
  \item[RQ2] How does KT behave when the supplied KC representation differs
    from the generator ontology?
  \item[RQ3] Can observable learner-response evidence recover an appropriate
    KC representation when generator truth is hidden?
  \item[RQ4] How do alternative representations generalise from seen grammar
    to unseen combinations and unseen values?
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions are: (i) a versioned benchmark constructed from every EGP
record under an explicit English verbal-morphosyntax scope; (ii) an
outcome-independent 18-KC generator ontology, 113-item bank, audited full-rank
\qstar{}, and 283,000-event learner stream; (iii) controlled full-dataset
studies of granularity, Q-matrix noise, discovery, linguistic generalisation,
and mastery recovery; and (iv) compact simulator and collection-design
controls that identify which assumptions carry the conclusions.  The result is
an executable methodological test bed.  Automatic linguistic judgments and
synthetic learner dynamics remain explicit limitations.
