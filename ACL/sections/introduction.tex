\section{Introduction}

Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates changing learner knowledge from chronological
responses \citep{corbett-anderson-1995,abdelrahman-etal-2023}.  Its input is
usually treated as a sequence of items, outcomes, and knowledge-component (KC)
labels.  Yet the label matrix embeds a measurement theory: it declares what
knowledge is reusable, what an item requires, and where practice should
transfer.  A large response log cannot by itself reveal whether those
declarations are valid.

This problem is acute in language learning.  In a dialogue corpus such as the
Teacher--Student Chatroom Corpus v2, a turn may be explanation, elicitation,
repair, clarification, or several grammatical phenomena rather than one
scorable opportunity \citep{caines-etal-2022-teacher}.  Large platform logs
record behaviour selected by a curriculum or adaptive policy, while open
responses admit alternative correct forms and structured errors.  The true
opportunity boundary, KC ontology, Q-matrix, and learner state remain latent.
Consequently, real logs cannot directly answer whether a recovered ontology is
the generating one, whether a KC split is absorbing item difficulty, or what
would have happened under a counterfactual measurement policy.

We study a perfect-information thought experiment.  What analyses would be
possible if a language-learning dataset supplied true grammatical
opportunities, generator KCs, Q, and learner mastery?  Because those quantities
are prohibitively difficult or impossible to observe jointly in human data, we
construct them synthetically.  The synthetic world provides experimental
truth---not human cognitive truth.

Perfect annotation creates internal validity but does not solve measurement.
If a learner cannot infer what response is requested, if several reasonable
answers are marked wrong, or if a false KC split captures a task-format effect,
then a mathematically convenient benchmark can still mislead.  We therefore
separate five layers:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\grammarcell &\rightarrow (\kstar,\qstar)
\rightarrow \text{instrument} \\
\text{instrument} &\rightarrow \text{policy + learner}
\rightarrow (\khat,\qhat) .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
A \grammarcell{} is a canonical construction; \kstar{} is a declared latent
coordinate system possessed by synthetic learners; and \khat{} is a downstream
hypothesis.  Thus \grammarcell{} $\ne$ \kstar{} $\ne$ \khat{}.

Our immutable reference, \texttt{grammar\_kt\_full\_v1}, processes every
English Grammar Profile record under an explicit verbal-morphosyntax scope
\citep{okeeffe-mark-2017}.  It freezes 75 cells, 18 generator factors, 113
prompts, a rank-18 $113\times18$ \qstar{}, and 283,000 binary events before any
downstream experiment.  It is a clean Q-driven laboratory environment: prompt
text is auditable, but the response simulator consumes only Q and mastery.

We ask three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[RQ1] What must be declared and audited to construct a grammar-learning
    measurement environment with known latent structure, and where does a clean
    Q-driven benchmark fall short?
  \item[RQ2] How do KC/Q misspecification and item- or format-specific nuisance
    affect prediction and recovery of the planted learner state?
  \item[RQ3] Which item-bank contrasts, assignment histories, and response
    information make latent structure and mastery distinguishable?
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions are fourfold.  First, we preserve a replayable clean-control
benchmark whose oracle truth supports counterfactual representation and
identifiability studies unavailable in real logs.  Second, we conduct a
complete learner/platform audit, a pedagogical KC audit, and repeated
outcome-blind KC induction; these expose substantive validity limits that Q
rank alone cannot answer.  Third, we attempt a full-rank matched-format bank and
retain its failure---only 5/38 complete families pass---rather than silently
relaxing the release gate.  Fourth, we isolate planted format/item nuisance,
learner heterogeneity, structured errors, assignment policies, and dialogue
openness in separate controlled studies.  The result is a methodology for
asking when KT is informative or misleading.  It does not claim a deployable
platform dataset, human-like simulator, or uniquely correct grammar ontology.
