Language-learning logs rarely expose trustworthy ground truth for grammatical
opportunity boundaries, knowledge components (KCs), item--KC mappings, or
learner mastery.  Synthetic data can expose those quantities, but known latent
truth does not make the observable measurement valid.  We build and preserve a
clean grammar-KT benchmark from all 1,222 records of a grammar resource: 75
canonical constructions, 18 declared generator factors, 113 prompts, a
full-rank Q-matrix, and 283,000 interactions from 1,000 synthetic learners.
Its known truth enables counterfactual studies unavailable in ordinary logs:
KC coarsening/refinement, Q misspecification, ontology equivalence, state
recovery, and collection geometry.  We then audit the measurement process
rather than assuming it is realistic.  Two model-based item audits agree that
only 60/113 prompts are usable as stored and flag 18 critical answer-space or
withhold cases; three outcome-blind KC inductions share only nine activation
signatures.  A preregistered four-format bank construction passes all gates for
only 5/38 matched families, so we release no ``realistic'' successor dataset.
In a separate content-free structural control, planted format nuisance makes a
false format-specific KC split relatively more predictive (three-seed
difference-in-differences $-.0316$); an explicit format covariate outperforms
that split.  Oracle-linked structured errors greatly improve failed-KC
localisation but yield only small next-response gains.  A dialogue continuum
improves visible naturalness while sharply reducing answer determinacy and KC
attribution.  The resulting contribution is not a synthetic human clone: it is
an auditable analysis of how KC structure, measurement nuisance, response
information, and assignment policy determine what grammar KT can infer.
